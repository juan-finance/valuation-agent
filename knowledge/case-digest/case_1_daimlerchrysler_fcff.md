# case_1_daimlerchrysler_fcff

**Daimler Chrysler**

Daimler Chrysler is a mature firm in a mature industry. Consequently, the existing market share and level of competition limit the growth opportunities in the industry. The company can be considered a relatively new organization, with two different cultures regarding the use of debt. Daimler has traditionally been more conservative and bank-oriented than Chrysler; the debt ratio will probably change over time. You can assume that the long-term growth rate is 3%, the long-term German bond rate is 4.87%, and the market risk premium is 4%. The bottom-up (unlevered) beta for the automobile industry is 0.61, and the market values of Equity and Debt are E62.3b and E64.488b, respectively. There are 913,714 outstanding shares, and Daimler Chrysler is rated AAA (default spread=0.2%). The price-to-book ratio is around 2.4 for the Auto Truck and Automobile industry.

- What method would you use for the evaluation?

- What are the reinvestment needs of the company, as a proportion of after-tax operating income?

- What are the expected Cash Flows?

- What is the appropriate discount rate?

- If the market price is E62.2, what investment strategy would you suggest?

**Solution**

- What method would you use for the evaluation?

Leverage policies seem to be rather unstable and they are expected to change in the future. This makes Dividend discount or FCFE models inaccurate in valuation. In addition, expected growth is 3%<stable growth rate (in developed markets around 4%). Consequently we can assume that the company is on a stable growth stage. A stable growth FCFF model should be more appropriate.

- What is the appropriate discount rate?

In our estimates we consider the Minority Interest as capital similar to equity. Therefore, all estimates must be adjusted for the value of minority interest. Ideally, this is done using the market value of this source of capital. The usual way is by using the M/B multiple.

MVMinority Interest=BVMinority Interest*MB=691*2.4=€1.658

Then we continue with the other inputs, such as the cost of equity

βlevered=β1+1-TDE=0.611+1-0.469464.488(62.3+1.658)=0.9363
ke=rf+β*RP=0.0487+0.9373*0.04=8.61%

And wacc

wacc=MVE+MVMIMVE+MVMI+MVDke+MVDMVE+MVMI+MVDkd(1-T)
=62.3+1.65862.3+1.658+64.50.0861+64.562.3+1.658+64.5(0.0487+0.002)(1-0.4694)
=0.0564 or 5.64%

- If the market price is E62.2, what investment strategy would you suggest?

PVFCFF=ExpectedFCFFwacc-g=2,9570.0564-0.03=112,008

This is the value of operating assets. We need to add the value of cash equivalents (assume no discount) to reach the value of the firm.

MVAssets=112,008+18,068=130,076

In order to reach the value of equity we need to take out the market value of debt and minority interests
Equity=130,076-64,488-1,658=63,929
ValueperShare=63,929m913,714=E69.96

Market price is lower than what we think is the fair value. Consequently we should expect it to go up. We should buy. Don t forget to discuss the efficient market hypothesis, the implied growth rate and the circularity issue.
