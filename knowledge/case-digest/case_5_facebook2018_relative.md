# case_5_facebook2018_relative

**Facebook 2018**

Facebook in 2017 and 2018 has had a rather challenging year. First the revelation of the abuse of its platform and interference with the American presidential election and then the scandal with the data abuse of Cambridge Analytica. Both issues has caused a rapid drop in the use on the number of the Monthly Active Users (MAU), which in many ways is the major element of stock pricing for social networks. Let s try to estimate a “fair” value for Facebook in March 2018, during a period, when both issues are at their peak. Is Facebook fairly priced, or is the bad media a hype?

Analysts’ forecasts:

We will make the following assumptions (explicitly). The revenue of Facebook will continue increasing (mainly due to Instagram and WhatsApp), but we also assume that they will offer (as they actually do) a revised data protection policy and therefore they will come back to higher growth from 2019-2020. This is also due to the fact that the majority of MAU is older than 25 years old (which is a slower pace age group) and that the portfolio of investments has widen considerably over the last years.

We assume the following: Growth of MAU will be 0 in the following year, but it will continue to be high (about 10%) for the following 4 years (up to year 5). Then it will start decreasing 2% up until it will reach a long term stable growth of 1%. We also assume that the Revenue/MAU (40,653/2,130≈$19) will remain stable and growth will be driven by the #MAU. On the long term (stable growth phase) growth in NI will reach fundamental growth of 5%. In addition, we will assume that although the Net Profit Margin (NPM), which has improved lately (39%), could not be very far from the industry average (13.8%) or the long term average of Facebook (21%). We assume that it will stay at 39% for the next 5 years and then it will gradually decrease (3% a year) till it will reach the long term average of Facebook at 21%. In brief:

It would be better to be used only in non-profitable firms. The P/E valuation here provides wide, but very consistent results with the MCAP at the moment. Therefore, the asset, according to market expectations, seems to be rather “fairly” priced. However, this valuations cannot be supported by our DCF assumptions. In order to check the validity of our DCF assumptions it would be worth to compute the implied rates that would make the valuation equal to the current MCAP ($464.19b).
