# case_4_tsingtao_fcfe_3stage

**Tsingtao Breweries **

Tsingtao Breweries produces and distributes beer and other alcoholic beverages in China and around the world under the Tsingtao brand name. It is a relatively small business that has 653.15 million outstanding shares listed on the Shanghai and Hong Kong Exchanges. The beer market in China and South East Asia is still underdeveloped, offering high growth opportunities. Tsingtao reports earnings equal to 72.36 million CY (Chinese Yuan) on a Book value of equity of 2,588 million CY. Tsingtao is forward looking and is undertaking significant new investments, which are financed using both new equity and debt. Debt issues have been relatively volatile, but firm aims at a relatively stable leverage, by issuing an equivalent number of shares, at least on the long term. These investments are expected to increase ROE to 12% within the first 5 years and to 20% the following 5 years (from +6 to +10) and stay there. Revenues, Capital Expenditure, Depreciations, Net Working Capital (non-cash) and Debt Ratio of the last 5 years are reported below (in million CY):

The risk-free rate in nominal CY (also the long term stable growth rate) is 10%, the Market risk premium of US is 4%, while the country risk premium for China is 2.28%. The beta for Tsingtao that reflects the bottom-up beta for the breweries sector in China is 0.75. As the market matures and reaches a stable growth stage analysts forecast that the beta will drift up to 0.80 and will stay at this level after 10 years. This is partially derived from estimation that Chinese economy will significantly improve within these 10 years and the country risk premium will drop (from +6 to +10 years) to 0.95% and it will be stabilized at this level.

You are an investor in France and you are considering including Tsingtao’s stock in your portfolio. You want to find the price you should be willing to pay for each stock (fair value for the Equity of the firm).

- What method would you use for the evaluation?

- What is the reinvestment ratio and the growth rate of the firm?

- What are the expected Cash Flows?

- What is the appropriate discount rate?

- What investment strategy would you follow if the market price of Tsingtao shares was 10.10 CY. How would you explain the difference?

**Solution**

- What method would you use for the evaluation?

We are required to find the value of Equity, so the choice should be between Dividends and FCFE. Corporate governance in China tends to be rather weak and Dividends do not always reflect the true FCFE. Consequently, it is better to employ a FCFE model. Investments and debt issues are rather volatile, and therefore, CFs need to be adapted. Please note that under unstable leverage a FCFF model might be more appropriate, since it incorporates changes in the D/E ratio into the discount rate and interest payments do not need to be scaled or normalized in the CFs. However, we are required to find the value of the equity and we know that firm aims at a relatively stable D/E ratio on the long term. This means that Debt ratio is not expected to change dramatically over the years, although reinvestment (both the rate and the actual figures) might be even more volatile.

In addition, Tsingtao is a relatively small company that tries to serve a potentially huge and growing market. Consequently, there are significant growth opportunities in the first years, which are expected to go through a moderate and then reach a relatively stable stage. Consequently, a three-stage growth model would better reflect CF and growth rate evolution, and thus the true value of the firm.
