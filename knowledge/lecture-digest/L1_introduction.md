## Slide 25

1. Discounted Cash flows (DCF)

The value of the asset is the present value of future cash flows
We consider Cash flows to equity (FCFE) or to the firm (FCFF)
Some of the FCF need to be positive, for an asset to have value
Assets that generate cash earlier, have higher value
Inputs
Life of the asset
Cash Flows
Discount rate
Philosophical basis
Every asset has a(n) (intrinsic) value, based on fundamentals
Markets are assumed to be efficient over time (not immediately) incorporating new information.

CF is Cash Flow, r is the discount rate and n is the life of the project

## Slide 29

2. Relative Valuation

The value of the asset can be found by looking at the market prices of “similar” or “comparable” assets.
 Inputs
Group of “similar” or “comparable” assets
Standardized measure of value
If assets are not comparable: “Control” variables
Philosophical basis
The “true” value of an asset is impossible to estimate. The value we can observe is the price the market is willing to pay.
Markets are assumed to make mistakes, which are easier spotted in comparable assets and they are more quickly corrected.

## Slide 33

Real Options-Further Remarks

Examples
Direct
Listed options
Warrants (call, firm)
Contingent Value Rights (put, firm)
Scores and LEAPs (long term calls, market)
Indirect (Real Options)
Equity in deeply troubled firm
Reserves owned by natural resource firms
Patent rights/ exclusive license 
Right to expand
Advantages
Allow for valuation of firms that we could not evaluate (non-comparable, non-profitable, high growth)
Can deal with uncertainty and thus, they increase value
Disadvantages
Many inputs are difficult to obtain (B&S formula)
Underlying asset needs to be evaluated first
Double-counting-Discount Rates and options
