# Goal
Final recommendation for investors with medium to low technical knowledge. This is an
academic exercise (the professor will calibrate this stage later), so keep the structure
easy to change.

# Required content
1. DCF: scenario analysis (bear/base/bull) and sensitivity to the main inputs (e.g. a
   tornado chart and a WACC vs growth table). You decide which inputs matter and say why.
2. Relative valuation: scenario analysis too (e.g. peer quartiles, alternative weights).
3. Combine both. Identify DCF assumptions that would bring the DCF closer to the relative
   valuation, and where the two overlap. Summarize in a table: DCF assumptions vs the
   assumptions implied by relative valuation, and the factors that make them differ.
4. Visual scale with the minimum and maximum valuations from stages 2 and 3.
5. Assess which scenario is most likely. Show a figure with your recommended valuation range.
6. Compare with analyst recommendations from highly trustworthy sources only (the exchanges,
   Thomson Reuters/LSEG, Bloomberg, Morningstar). Say which sources you could access.
7. Final Buy/Hold/Sell with a recommendation thermometer figure and a short text. The text
   must explain WHY, tie to the narrative of the annual report or strategy, and point to
   one parameter (margin, growth, cost of equity, etc.) that the market may be mispricing.

# Rules
- Plain-English summary first. Define every term. Add a glossary.
- Include the disclaimer: educational analysis, not investment advice.
- Run methodology/qa_checklist.md before delivering. Report data gaps.
- Outputs: report.pdf (per methodology/report_template.md), model.xlsx, all figures as PNG.
