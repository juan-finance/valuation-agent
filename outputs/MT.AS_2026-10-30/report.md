## 3. Industry and competitors

Steel is cyclical, capital-intensive and exposed to construction, automotive demand, trade rules, energy, ore and carbon costs. These mechanisms are risks, not quantitative forecasts of tariffs or steel prices. EU import protection could improve spreads; higher energy costs, weak demand or decarbonization investment could offset it. The peer set spans steel, metals/mining and three more distant process-industry businesses. Comparability is therefore limited even before the outstanding ownership/event checks.

## 9. Reconciliation and market expectations

The three point estimates are independent and were frozen before choosing the final method. DCF values consolidated operating cash flows, subtracts reinvestment and values associates separately. Peer PBV includes associates in parent book/earnings and is strongly affected by reported NI; scaling PBV by ROE is closely related to PE, not a wholly independent cash-flow test. The published PBV fit has an intercept and rewards book ROE/growth without explicitly forecasting steel reinvestment. It can remain high even after a target earnings stress.
The vendor FY25 EBIT1667 versus issuer3628 (USDm) is a1961 difference. Classification of the1858 bargain gain is a plausible contributor, not a verified full bridge. Uniform vendor inputs have not been overwritten with issuer EBIT. Different denominators, scopes, fiscal year ends, shares and historical FX matter. PBV uses FY25 closing equity54466; DCF uses latest June equity/claims for its bridge. The relative basic-share denominator differs from the DCF diluted estimate by0.18%, much too little to explain the valuation gap.
Holding other DCF inputs fixed, the current price requires a terminal/midcycle EBITDA margin around19.1%, versus14.89% selected. Peer and regression prices imply still stronger recovery under the DCF mechanics (method_reconciliation.csv). These are reverse-model implications, not a claim that the market literally forecasts those margins. Lower reinvestment, faster growth or lower discount rates could also support higher prices. A return to more normal spreads may already be in peer prices. No trusted, cutoff-aligned consensus forecast has been obtained, so no market consensus growth is asserted.

## 11. Risks and what changes the view

A sustainably higher margin or lower capital burden could invalidate the SELL model. A renewed steel upcycle, import protection, profitable project ramp-ups and buybacks support the bull case; energy inflation, demand weakness, carbon costs, commodity exposure, project overruns, pension/associate impairments and currency moves threaten it. The15-year cash-generation profile has not been independently verified. Revisit after official cutoff shares/claims and the FY25 vendor/issuer bridge are resolved. A verified set of near-pure steel peers and uniform normalized earnings could materially change the relative evidence. No short-sale suitability or position size is implied.

## 12. Glossary

EV: value of the operating business to all financiers. EBITDA: profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; definitions and adjustments differ. EBIT: operating profit. FCFF: operating cash after tax and reinvestment, before financing cash flows. WACC: weighted average cost of capital, used to discount FCFF. ROE: parent earnings divided by common equity; average equity for peers, closing equity for the published equation. ROIC/ROC: operating return on invested capital. NCI: claims owned by outside shareholders of consolidated subsidiaries. CRP: incremental country risk premium. Terminal value: value after the explicit forecast. PBV: market equity/book common equity. R-squared: variation explained in the historical published fit, not probability a price is right. Interquartile range:25th to75th percentile of a peer distribution.
